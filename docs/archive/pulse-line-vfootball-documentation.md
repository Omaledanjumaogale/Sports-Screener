# Pulse Line — Virtual Football Screener — Documentation v1

**File this document describes:** `pulse-line-vfootball-screener.html`
**Sibling projects:** this tool follows the same core architecture as `rally-line-tt-totals-screener.html` (table tennis) and `diamond-line-baseball-screener.html` (baseball) — config-driven market definitions, per-market normalized probability/margin/EV, a Match Shape Intelligence cross-check layer, and a Final Verdict panel. Where this document says "as in the earlier tools," the underlying mechanism is identical; what's different here is the deliberately **narrow** scope — see Part 1.
**Type:** single-file, offline-capable HTML/CSS/JS application, config-driven
**Purpose:** screen pre-round SportyBet Virtual Football odds for exactly five target selections, using a curated set of supporting markets purely as cross-checks

---

## PART 1 — Executive Summary: Why This Tool Is Narrow On Purpose

The table tennis and baseball tools in this project covered every commonly-offered market (14 and 21 respectively) because the brief for those asked for full market coverage. This tool does the opposite deliberately: **the brief for Virtual Football names exactly five outcomes as the only things this tool should ever recommend** — Match Over 1.5, Match Over 2.5, Home Team Over 0.5, Away Team Over 0.5, and BTTS. Every other market SportyBet's Virtual Football board offers (1X2, Handicap, Double Chance, Odd/Even, Correct Score, 1st Half 1X2/Handicap/Double Chance, the 1X2-&-GG/NG combo) is either left out entirely or included **only as a cross-check input**, never as something the tool will recommend on its own. Part 2 explains exactly what made the cut and why.

**Why narrow is the right call here, specifically:** Virtual Football rounds run roughly every three to five minutes. A tool that asked for fifteen markets' worth of odds before producing a read would be useless in practice — by the time you'd entered everything, the round would be over. Five direct target prices plus a handful of optional cross-check fields is something you can fill in during a single pre-round window. Depth was traded for speed on purpose, matching how this product is actually used.

**The honesty commitment carried over unchanged from the other two tools:** every probability here is a bookmaker price with margin removed, not an independent forecast. One thing is genuinely different about Virtual Football, though, and it's worth stating plainly: these matches are generated by the operator's own simulation engine, and the odds are set directly from that engine's own internal probability model. Real-sport odds sometimes drift from "true" probability because of public betting patterns, team news, or bookmaker risk management across thousands of bettors with opinions. Virtual Football odds have none of that — there is no public sentiment to move the line, no injury news, no sharp money. That means the margin-minimization and cross-market-consistency techniques this tool uses are, if anything, **more** honestly the whole story here than in real sports — there simply isn't a public-perception inefficiency to go looking for underneath the engine's own numbers.

## PART 2 — Market Inventory: What Made the Cut and Why

### The five target markets (Primary — the only verdict candidates)

| Market | Why it's one of the five |
|---|---|
| Match Goals — Over/Under 1.5 | Named directly in the brief |
| Match Goals — Over/Under 2.5 | Named directly in the brief |
| Home Team Goals — Over/Under 0.5 | Named directly in the brief ("team A... over 0.5") |
| Away Team Goals — Over/Under 0.5 | Named directly in the brief ("team B... over 0.5") |
| Both Teams To Score (GG/NG) | Named directly in the brief |

Each renders as a single fixed-line Over/Under (or Yes/No) entry — no dropdown, no tiers — because the line itself isn't a choice here, it's fixed by the brief. You enter both sides' prices; the tool tells you which side is favoured and by how much.

### The six supporting markets (Context — feed cross-checks, never the verdict)

| Market | What it's for |
|---|---|
| Match Goals — Extra Lines (0.5, 3.5, 4.5, 5.5, 6.5) | Lets the tool fit a fuller goals curve around the two target lines — see Part 4.2 |
| Home / Away Team Goals — Extra Lines (1.5, 2.5, 3.5) | Same purpose, per team |
| 1st Half — Total Goals O/U | Feeds the pace-share check (Part 4.4) |
| Match Result (1X2) | Shown as plain context (which side is favoured and by how much) — informative for reading the round, not wired into a specific flag |
| Correct Score Grid (16 scorelines + "Other") | The single richest supporting market — see Part 3 |

**What was deliberately left out entirely, and why:** Handicap, Double Chance, Odd/Even, all three 1st Half sub-markets other than the total (1X2, Double Chance, Handicap), and the 1X2-&-GG/NG combo. None of these add information that isn't already better captured by the markets kept — Double Chance and Handicap are both derivable in spirit from 1X2 and the goals markets already collected; Odd/Even doesn't bear on any of the five thresholded questions this tool answers; the 1st-Half sub-markets beyond the total duplicate information the 1st-Half Total and full 1X2 already provide for this tool's purposes. Leaving them out is the direct answer to the brief's instruction to "advise only the primary market options you need me to provide" — this list is that answer.

## PART 3 — The Correct Score Grid: A Joint Distribution, Not Just Another Market

This is the most powerful single input in the tool, and it works differently from every other market here. A Correct Score market prices every individual scoreline, which means — unlike any Over/Under market, which only tells you about one threshold — a sufficiently filled-in Correct Score grid tells you the **entire joint distribution** of home and away goals at once. From that one grid, the tool directly computes an independent estimate of all five target probabilities simultaneously:

- **Over 1.5** = the summed probability of every scoreline with 2+ combined goals
- **Over 2.5** = the summed probability of every scoreline with 3+ combined goals
- **Home Over 0.5** = the summed probability of every scoreline where the home team scored at least once
- **Away Over 0.5** = the summed probability of every scoreline where the away team scored at least once
- **BTTS — Yes** = the summed probability of every scoreline where both teams scored at least once

The grid covers the sixteen most common scorelines (0-0 through 3-3) plus a single **"Other"** field for everything outside that range — necessary because Virtual Football goal lines run unusually high (the sample odds went up to 9.5), so a meaningful chunk of probability can sit outside a 0–3-per-side grid. The "Other" bucket is folded into the Over 1.5 and Over 2.5 sums in full, since literally any scoreline outside the 16 listed has 4 or more combined goals and is guaranteed to clear both thresholds. It is **not** folded into the Home/Away/BTTS sums, because "Other" doesn't tell the tool how those goals split between the two teams — a scoreline like 4-0 and a scoreline like 4-4 both count as "Other," but only one of them is a BTTS winner. The tool labels any Home/Away/BTTS figure derived while "Other" is filled in as a **lower bound**, and says so directly next to the number, rather than quietly overstating it.

You don't need to fill in all sixteen cells for this to work — the tool starts computing once at least four cells (counting "Other") are filled — but accuracy improves with coverage, and the tool shows exactly how many of the seventeen fields you've used so you always know how much to trust the derived numbers.

## PART 4 — The Match Shape Intelligence Checks

Four distinct cross-checks, each comparing two independently-derived readings of the same underlying question — the same principle as the baseball tool's three checks, adapted to what's actually available on a Virtual Football board.

### 4.1 Correct Score vs. Direct Market (one check per target, up to five)

For each of the five target markets, if both a direct market price and a Correct-Score-derived figure are available, the tool compares them and flags anything more than a 10-point gap. This is the most direct application of the "does the book price its own markets consistently with each other" principle from the earlier tools — here it's especially clean, because the Correct Score market and the direct Over/Under markets are, in principle, pricing exactly the same underlying event.

### 4.2 Goals Curve Fit (the "fair number" technique, carried over from baseball)

Exactly the interpolation technique documented in the baseball tool (its Part 4.2): using every line you've entered for a given quantity — here, the match goals lines (0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5, 4.5, 5.5, 6.5, however many you fill in) — the tool finds the precise point where the market's own Over probability crosses 50%, using linear interpolation between whichever two entered lines bracket that point. This "fair match-goals line" is a more precise summary of the market's own view than reading any single line alone, and it's shown directly in the intelligence panel.

### 4.3 Team Goals Sum vs. Match Total

The same fair-number technique applied separately to the Home Team goals lines and the Away Team goals lines; their sum is compared against the fair match-goals line from 4.2. In principle these should closely agree, since a combined match-total market and the sum of two team-total markets price the same underlying quantity. A gap of half a goal (0.5) or more is flagged.

### 4.4 First-Half Pace Share

The fair number from the 1st Half Total Goals market, divided by the fair number from the full match goals curve, expressed as a percentage. There's no fixed "correct" band asserted here the way the baseball tool asserts one for its pace-ratio check (Virtual Football's underlying scoring-rate model isn't something this project has independent data on) — the figure is shown as plain context in the intelligence grid for you to interpret, rather than auto-flagged against a threshold that would be no better than a guess.

**The same honesty caveat as the baseball tool's checks applies word for word here:** none of these four checks tell you what will happen in the match. They tell you whether the bookmaker's own markets — all set by the same simulation engine — are internally consistent with each other. Consistency is mildly reassuring; a flagged divergence means "look closer before committing to either side," never "bet against the book."

## PART 5 — The Calculation Model (shared mechanics)

Identical mechanics to the table tennis and baseball tools:

- **Normalization**: `pA = (1/oddsA) / (1/oddsA + 1/oddsB) × 100` for every two-way market. The 1X2 context market is normalized three ways using the same generalized approach.
- **Margin**: `margin% = (Σ 1/oddsᵢ − 1) × 100`, shown next to every market's top pick — the one number in the tool representing genuine, prediction-free value (lower margin costs less to bet, full stop).
- **Market-Fair EV**: `EV = (probability/100 × odds) − 1`, using a market's own normalized probability against its own raw price. See Part 7 before treating this as a profit forecast.

## PART 6 — The Final Verdict

Same mechanism as the other two tools, scaled to five candidates instead of eight or fourteen: the top pick from each of the five Primary target markets becomes a verdict candidate.

- **Safest Selection** = the single highest normalized-probability candidate across all five.
- **Best Value Selection** = among candidates at 55%+ probability, the one with the least-negative EV (lowest margin).
- A ranked table of all five candidates shows exactly how close the alternatives were — useful here in particular, since Virtual Football's high-scoring format often means several of the five targets read as strongly favoured at once, and the table makes it easy to see which one is favoured by the widest margin rather than just which one crosses 50% first.

## PART 7 — What EV and the Consistency Checks Actually Mean (read before staking anything)

Identical caveat to the other two tools in this project, restated for this context: a market-fair EV figure computed from a market's own de-vigged odds against its own raw price will always land at or near zero — it is a margin-cost indicator, not a forecast. The Correct Score cross-check, the goals-curve fit, the team-totals check, and the pace-share figure are all ways of asking "does this simulation engine's own board agree with itself," not ways of predicting the outcome of a randomly-generated match. Given that Virtual Football odds are set directly by the engine with no public-perception layer to exploit (Part 1), it is especially important not to over-read a consistency flag here as "the book got this one wrong" — it is far more likely to simply reflect how the operator manages margin across a compact set of correlated markets on a fast-moving product.

## PART 8 — Auto-Save Behavior

Identical mechanism to the other two tools: every input is written to `localStorage` under the key `pulseLineVF_v1_state` and reloaded automatically the next time the file is opened directly in a standard browser (double-click, `file://`, or your own simple web server). It will not persist inside a sandboxed in-chat preview pane for the same security reasons noted in the earlier tools' documentation — the app detects this gracefully and simply skips saving/loading without breaking anything else. Nothing is cleared automatically within a session; only the reset button clears it.

## PART 9 — Limitations

1. **This tool answers exactly five questions and no others, by design.** If you want a read on 1X2, Handicap, Double Chance, or any other Virtual Football market, this is not the tool for it — see Part 1 and Part 2 for why that's intentional rather than an oversight.
2. **The Correct Score grid's Home/Away/BTTS figures are a lower bound whenever the "Other" bucket carries meaningful probability** (Part 3) — always check the tool's own note on this before trusting a Correct-Score-derived figure for those three targets.
3. **The 1st-half pace share (Part 4.4) is shown without a judged "typical" band**, unlike the baseball tool's pace-ratio check, because this project has no independent data on Virtual Football's own scoring-distribution-by-half to calibrate a threshold against.
4. **No independent probability source exists anywhere in this tool.** Every number is derived from odds you typed in from the same simulation engine's own board — see Part 1's discussion of what that does and doesn't mean for finding value.
5. **The BTTS independence estimate (Part 4's core BTTS check) assumes each team's scoring is statistically independent of the other's**, which is a simplification — real and simulated match dynamics can correlate the two (a team that concedes early may open up and concede again, or a team already leading comfortably may ease off). Treat it as a sanity-check anchor, not a superior estimate to the market's own direct GG/NG price.
6. **No live odds feed, no round history, no simulation-engine internals.** Every read is price-only and manual, by design, consistent with the rest of this project's offline-first scope.

## PART 10 — How-To-Use Guide

1. Open `pulse-line-vfootball-screener.html` in any modern browser — no internet connection needed after the first load.
2. Enter home/away team names (optional, cosmetic) — useful since Virtual Football fixtures recycle real club names.
3. Fill in the five **Target Markets** first — this alone is enough to get a Final Verdict.
4. If you have time before the round starts, open the **Supporting Markets** section and add whichever of the six context markets your bookmaker screen shows — the Correct Score grid gives the biggest boost to confidence per field filled in.
5. Check the **Match Shape Intelligence** panel for the fair-number readings and any consistency flags.
6. Scroll to the **Final Verdict** panel for the safest pick, the lowest-vig alternative among the five, and the full ranked table.
7. Read the EV column as "margin cost," not "expected profit" — Part 7.
8. Use "Clear all fields & saved data" between rounds — Virtual Football moves fast, so a clean slate matters.

## PART 11 — Disclaimers (must remain in any distributed version)

This tool is a decision-support screening aid limited by design to five specific Virtual Football markets. It normalizes and cross-checks odds you provide; it does not access the simulation engine's internals, does not possess an independent statistical model of either team, and does not guarantee any outcome, win rate, or profit. The EV figures reflect bookmaker margin embedded in the prices you entered, not a forecast. The Match Shape Intelligence flags point out when a bookmaker's own correlated markets disagree with each other — they are not instructions to bet either side of a divergence, and Virtual Football odds in particular are set directly by the operator's simulation engine with no independent public-perception layer to find an edge in. No claim in this tool or its documentation should be read as a promise that it can consistently beat a bookmaker's margin; that is not something any odds-only tool can do. Users are solely responsible for their own staking decisions. If betting stops being enjoyable or feels out of control, free, confidential support is available in most regions through national gambling-help helplines.
